# Study design {#sec:design}

## Datasets and tasks

Three public datasets were used (@tbl:datasets). BDG2 [@miller2020; @bdg2data2020], two years of hourly meter data from North America and Europe, carries the energy results; its authors' cleaned electricity meters were used, and buildings with at least 90% of hourly readings at sites with weather data were eligible (1,256 buildings at 16 sites). The Room-level Occupancy and Building Operation Dataset (ROBOD) from Singapore [@tekler2022; @robod2022data] and data of six office rooms and their building systems in Aalborg, Denmark [@melgaard2024; @aalborg2024data] add indoor-environment, occupancy and system-level energy targets, which matter for energy through demand-controlled ventilation, pre-cooling and setbacks. A script downloads and checksum-verifies all three, so every result can be reproduced; the raw data are not redistributed. The record of Building 59 at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory [@luo2022] was not used, because its repository does not allow scripted download.

BDG2 contributes one task per building, hourly electricity 24 h ahead, with one configuration decided by the agent for the whole dataset; as the dataset has no weather forecasts, weather enters only up to the forecast time. ROBOD contributes forecasts 30 min ahead of the room variables, forecasts 1 h ahead of chilled-water and plug-load energy, and a virtual people counter that estimates the current count from Wi-Fi connections, CO~2~, sound, light and plug loads. The Aalborg data contribute forecasts 1 h ahead of the room variables and of ventilation and district-heating power, the latter also 24 h ahead. All tasks use 15-min resolution (hourly for BDG2), the default 70/15/15% time-ordered split and small hyperparameter searches: ridge regression and two gradient-boosting models, one predicting the level and one the change from the latest value (logistic regression and gradient boosting for binary targets), with three settings each, and for ROBOD also a random forest and six settings. The models are therefore deliberately ordinary: the results show how often a routinely configured model is worth deploying against strong simple rules, not how good the best possible model is.

## Pre-specified analysis plan

Every experiment was specified in an analysis plan committed to the repository, together with the frozen package version, before any of them was run (case_studies/ANALYSIS_PLAN_v3.md; the commit history records the order). The plan fixes the samples and seeds, the baselines, the arms, the outcomes and the thresholds, and states that every number is reported as computed. One amendment was made before any result was used: a smoke test showed that the first version of the TOWT baseline was numerically unstable when a temperature segment had few training hours, and the corrected version (0.3.1) produced every result reported. The BDG2 sample is the first 200 buildings of a fixed order, in which the eligible buildings were shuffled with seed 0 and taken round-robin over sites (4 to 15 buildings per site). The experiments are numbered here in the order of the research questions; the plan labels the M&V, protocol, lifecycle and fault experiments E5, E2, E4 and E3. Analyses not in the plan are marked as exploratory.

## Roles of the agent and the author

An LLM agent (Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic, operated in Claude Code) followed the Agent Skill to load the data, decode the point names, write the declarations and make the judgment calls, for example reading the undocumented units of the Aalborg data from the magnitudes of the values and excluding the presence flag of ROBOD from the inputs of the people counter because it derives from the same camera ground truth. These decisions are frozen in the case-study scripts and copied into every run record; the agent's transcripts were not archived. The same agent wrote the package and the analysis scripts, so the case studies show the framework operated by an agent, not an independent test of agents (@sec:validity). The author set the research questions, the datasets and the analysis plan, reviewed the code, the analyses and the text, and traced every reported number to the result files with an automated tool.

Several detectors were revised on ROBOD and Aalborg data during development (Supplementary Material, Table S1), so the data checks are partly in-sample there. BDG2 prompted no revision and its configuration was fixed before its first run, but it is not strictly held out, because its first 20 buildings were analyzed in an earlier version of this study. On ROBOD, the chilled-water meters read zero for whole days while the air-handling unit (AHU) ran with its cooling-coil valve open (9 days in Room 3, 1 in Room 4 and 10 in Room 5); the case-study agent missed this fault, independent agent instances found it (Supplementary Material, Section S3), and the loader sets those days to missing.

## Experiments {#sec:experiments}

E1 (RQ1, portfolio and many targets). All 272 tasks are run through the full workflow. The outcomes are skill against the strongest baseline, a Diebold–Mariano (DM) test against it [@diebold1995], a day-block bootstrap interval for skill, the Guideline 14 results, the interval coverage and the gate decisions, also under stricter gates.

E2 (RQ2, M&V pseudo-savings). For the BDG2 sample buildings with at least 90% of readings in both years, 2016 is the baseline period and 2017 the reporting period. No efficiency measures are known to have been installed, so the true savings are taken to be zero and any predicted savings are error [@granderson2016accuracy]. The framework runs in estimation mode (concurrent weather and calendar, no lags of the metered target), selects among ridge regression, gradient boosting and its baselines on a time-ordered split within 2016, and refits the chosen configuration on all of 2016; a TOWT regression fitted on all of 2016 is the M&V reference. The outcome is the annual pseudo-savings error, predicted minus metered 2017 consumption divided by metered consumption. To link the protocol to M&V decisions, the error of TOWT is also estimated from 2016 in four ways (in-sample, shuffled and blocked five-fold cross-validation, and the time-ordered split) and compared with the error realized in 2017. Buildings whose 2017 mean load differs from the 2016 mean by more than 30% are flagged as likely non-routine changes.

E3 (RQ3, evaluation protocol). The same random forest is evaluated with shuffled five-fold cross-validation, equivalent to the random split of the preliminary study; with feature selection on all rows before shuffling, its full recipe, and, as a control, inside each fold; with blocked five-fold cross-validation, which removes from training a buffer of the longest input look-back plus the horizon around each test block; and with forward chaining, which tests blocked folds 2–5 with training on earlier data only [@tashman2000out]. Errors are compared on the same rows of folds 2–5. E3 uses the 22 regression tasks of ROBOD Rooms 4 and 5 and of Aalborg Room A and its systems, and the first 40 BDG2 buildings.

E4 (RQ4, lifecycle and gate audit). For the first 50 BDG2 sample buildings with at least 90% of readings in both years, the day-ahead task is deployed with a model trained on 2016 and operated through 2017. One model is fitted per month on all earlier data, so that every policy is simulated from the same models: static; monthly; quarterly; drift-triggered retraining at tolerances of 1.1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2.0 (the error-ratio and input-range triggers of @sec:gates); gated retraining at 1.5, in which the challenger must also pass the gates and beat the champion on rows neither has trained on; the same with a fallback that serves persistence in the month after the model lost to it; and persistence. The outcomes are the mean monthly and pooled 2017 RMSE per building. In the gate audit, each monthly model's gate decision is compared with its realized skill against its own reference over the following three months. The earlier Aalborg lifecycle case (district heating and Room A CO~2~, May to December 2023) is repeated on the frozen version.

E5 (RQ5, data faults). Eight known faults are planted at random positions in the Aalborg Room A data of February to June 2023, with ten seeds: a change from °C to °F in the last 40% of the zone temperature, a three-day stuck meter, ten spikes, twenty −999 placeholders in the target, twenty "OFFLINE" text entries, a 12-h outage, a day of duplicated rows and thirty rows out of order. A fault counts as detected when the check reports an issue of that type on that column that it did not report on the clean data. The same random forest, forecasting CO~2~ 1 h ahead, is trained on clean data prepared by the framework and by a quick script that coerces text, sorts, averages duplicates, resamples and fills forward; on faulty data prepared by the quick script, with and without a unit fix; by a competent script that also drops sentinel values and impossible readings and fills only gaps of up to 1 h, with the unit fix; and by the framework after the same unit fix, coded by the author, resolves its one blocking error. All arms are scored against the clean target over the same test period. Warnings raised on the untouched data of all three datasets are counted by type.

A formative usability study, in which fresh agent instances built two models with and without the framework, is reported in the Supplementary Material (Section S3); with one run per cell, it answers no research question.

## Statistical analysis

The DM test compares the losses of the model and of the reference on the test window through the loss differential $d_t = e_{t,\mathrm{model}}^2 - e_{t,\mathrm{ref}}^2$ (Brier losses for binary targets), whose mean over the $n$ test observations is $\bar{d}$,

$$ \mathrm{DM} = \frac{\bar{d}}{\sqrt{\hat{\sigma}^2_{\mathrm{HAC}}/n}} $$ {#eq:dm}

where $\hat{\sigma}^2_{\mathrm{HAC}}$ is a heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent variance with a Bartlett kernel and a lag equal to the larger of the horizon and the Newey–West bandwidth $\lfloor 4(n/100)^{2/9} \rfloor$ [@newey1994automatic]. A model is counted as better than its reference when DM < 0 with a two-sided p-value below 0.05 from the normal distribution; the small-sample correction of Harvey et al. [@harvey1997testing] and a Benjamini–Hochberg correction [@benjamini1995controlling] across tasks are sensitivity analyses. Skill intervals for single tasks come from a bootstrap of whole test days (500 resamples). Because tasks share rooms, systems and test windows, shares of tasks have 95% intervals from a cluster bootstrap over rooms, building systems and BDG2 buildings (2,000 resamples). Retraining policies are compared with a bootstrap over buildings, and fault arms that share a seed with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.
