What worked: check_data caught most real problems on the raw frame (-999 CO2, the 1521 ppm needle, 'OFFLINE' text, unsorted rows, the 288 duplicated rows, the 12 h gap, the degF stretch in room temperature) and recognised the CO2/wind logging as change-of-value with a deadband (flat_ok=True worked once I used the dict form). Whole check/prepare/fit/save_run took ~80 s. Problems and gaps, all hit in this run: (1) Timestamps with mixed UTC offsets (this export has +0100 and +0200 because of DST) make Project(..., timestamp='timestamp', timestamp_format='%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%S%z') raise a bare pandas 'ValueError: Mixed timezones detected. Pass utc=True...' (same with timestamp_format=None); schema.to_time_index does not use utc=True or convert to local time, so the user has to pre-parse; the skill's timestamp section covers UTC exports but not this common local-with-offset format, and the error is not wrapped with advice. (2) The room-temperature column switched from degC to degF on 12 May (21.8% of rows): check_data reports only 'out_of_range ... too many to be sensor glitches: check the declaration', with no date range, no hint that it is one contiguous stretch/unit change, and no unit_mismatch suggestion because most values fit degC. Declaring degF would make the other 78% wrong; there is no helper for per-segment unit conversion, so I converted per sample in my own loader. (3) The stuck-sensor rule is over-aggressive for sensors with a physical floor or COV logging: prepare() masked 9,759 of 26,916 (36%) solar-irradiance samples (the night-time floor of ~4 W/m2 repeated for 12 h) and 557 outdoor-temperature samples as 'stuck' with no deadband hint (the hint appeared for CO2 and wind but not for those), and silently forward-filled them up to 1 h; I had to know to declare flat_ok=True per column, and flat_ok is only available in the dict/ColumnSpec form (the shorthand string 'co2 [ppm]' cannot carry it and it is only described in the ColumnSpec docstring, not in SKILL.md). (4) The spring DST jump (01:55 -> 03:00) is merged into the 'gaps' warning (13 h 10 min = 12 h 5 min + 1 h 5 min) without saying it is the clock change. (5) fit() evaluates on one chronological test block (last ~15%, ~2 weeks, here late May): persistence RMSE was 29.5 ppm there but 46-48 ppm in April blocks, so a single block gives a very unstable headline number; there is no rolling-origin (multi-block) report even though TimeSeriesSplit is used internally for tuning. The ranking in that single window (persistence 26.3 > ridge 26.6 > gbm 29.7 on validation) says 'no ML beat persistence', whereas my own engineered features and an ExtraTrees delta model beat persistence by ~9% in a 3-block rolling-origin test (pooled 37.5 vs 41.3 ppm). (6) Feature engineering is limited to lags of declared columns (target lags 4-7 steps, 1 day, 1 week, 1-day mean) plus hour/dow: no rolling max/min, time-since-event, history-of-occupancy, bin median/last-value features and no hook for a custom estimator/feature builder, so the saved bemlops model could not be the final model; I would have to pre-compute those as 'other' columns and lose the guarantee that they are lag-safe (hard rule 2 then depends entirely on my code; I verified it with a truncation and a perturbation test instead). (7) The benchmark needs a forecast for intervals after the last timestamp in the file (live forecast) and an index per interval start; I could not tell from the docs whether ForecastModel.predict does exactly that for a file ending mid-interval, so I did not wrap it. (8) Not a bug but missing: no guidance in the skill on how to treat a People-counter that is only logged part of the time (I dropped it), and no 'regime change' check (the ventilation/occupancy regime changed ~14-24 Apr).
